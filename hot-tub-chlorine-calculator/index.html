How it’s worked out

Dichlor granules

Sodium dichlor (dihydrate) is 55.4 % available chlorine: 2 × 70.906 ÷ 255.98. So:

grams = FC rise (ppm) × litres ÷ 554

Each 1 ppm of FC from dichlor also adds 0.91 ppm of cyanuric acid (CYA), which stays in the water until you drain. Example: 1,500 L from 0 to 5 ppm takes 13.5 g and adds 4.6 ppm CYA.

Liquid chlorine and bleach

Liquid chlorine uses the TroubleFreePool density model, which accounts for how heavy stronger solutions are:

k = (75.71 p + 0.746 p²) × (1.02 − 0.008 p if p > 9) ppm per fl oz per US gallon, where p is the strength in %.

mL = FC rise × litres ÷ (k ÷ 7.8125). At 6 %, 1 mL in 1 L adds 61.6 ppm. Use plain, unscented bleach.

Cal-hypo

Calcium hypochlorite is weighed by its label strength (65 to 73 % available chlorine). Each 1 ppm of FC adds 0.71 ppm of calcium hardness.

Limits

The routine target is the top of the soak band, 3 to 5 ppm (PHTA). The calculator never plans above 10 ppm, the PHTA maximum and the level CDC uses for cleaning up a hot tub. With combined chlorine above 0.5 ppm, the shock target is 10 × CC, capped at 10. Stay out of the water until FC is back to 5 ppm or below, and follow the product label.

In the app

Tubwise picks the target from your system and your CYA, tracks the CYA every dichlor dose adds, and tells you when the water is fine to soak in again.
