How to test hot tub water with a drop kit

A drop kit is the most accurate test you can do at home. This is the full routine for a FAS-DPD kit of the Taylor K-2006 type, with the numbers each drop is worth. It takes about 10 minutes.

Before you start

  • You need a drop kit with FAS-DPD chlorine (for example Taylor K-2006, or K-2106 for bromine), clean sample tubes and a white background or daylight for colours.
  • Take the water from elbow depth, away from the jets and any floater or feeder. Rinse the tube with tub water first.
  • Hold each dropper bottle straight up, so every drop is the same size. Swirl after each drop and count out loud.
  • Testing a cold plunge? Let the sample warm to room temperature first; cold samples can change colour slowly.

Free and combined chlorine (FAS-DPD)

  1. Fill the tube to 25 mL. Use 10 mL instead if you expect more than about 10 ppm.
  2. Add the R-0870 powder (2 level dippers for the 25 mL sample) and swirl. Pink means there is chlorine.
  3. Add R-0871 one drop at a time, swirling, until the pink is gone. Count the drops.
  4. FC = drops × 0.2 with 25 mL, or drops × 0.5 with 10 mL.
  5. For combined chlorine, add 5 drops of R-0003 to the same sample. If it turns pink again, keep adding R-0871 until clear. CC = extra drops × the same factor

Example: 12 drops on a 25 mL sample = 2.4 ppm FC. If you used MPS (non-chlorine shock) in the last 24 hours, it can show up as CC.

Bromine (K-2106 type kits)

Same steps as free chlorine, with R-0872 as the titrant. Each drop is worth 0.5 ppm on a 25 mL sample, or 1.25 ppm on 10 mL. Example: 4 drops on 10 mL = 5.0 ppm bromine.

Only have a chlorine kit? Multiply the chlorine result by 2.25 to get bromine. The bromine converter does it for you.

pH

  1. Fill the pH comparator to 44 mL.
  2. Add 5 drops of R-0004, cap and turn it over a few times.
  3. Compare against the colour standards (7.0 to 8.0). If it sits between two, take the average.

Very high chlorine can bleach the pH colour. If FC is above 10 ppm, test pH again once it has dropped.

Total alkalinity (TA)

  1. Fill the tube to 25 mL. Add 2 drops of R-0007, then 5 drops of R-0008. The sample turns green.
  2. Add R-0009 one drop at a time until it turns red.
  3. TA = drops × 10 ppm

For high TA, use 10 mL with 1 drop of R-0007 and 3 drops of R-0008; each drop of R-0009 is then 25 ppm.

Calcium hardness (CH)

  1. Fill the tube to 25 mL. Add 20 drops of R-0010 and 5 drops of R-0011L. The sample turns red.
  2. Add R-0012 one drop at a time until it turns blue.
  3. CH = drops × 10 ppm

For hard water, use 10 mL with 10 drops of R-0010 and 3 drops of R-0011L; each drop is then 25 ppm.

Cyanuric acid (CYA), if you use dichlor

  1. Fill the mixing bottle to the 7 mL mark with tub water, then add R-0013 up to the 14 mL mark.
  2. Cap and mix for 30 seconds. The sample goes cloudy.
  3. Hold the view tube over the black dot on the label and pour the cloudy sample in slowly, looking straight down, until the dot disappears.
  4. Read the scale at the liquid level. It reads 30 to 100 ppm.

Dichlor adds CYA with every dose and only draining removes it. Above 40 ppm, plan a partial drain.

Adjust in this order: alkalinity, then pH, then sanitizer, with 20 minutes of jets between them (4 hours of filtering for an inflatable without jets). The pH and alkalinity calculator and the chlorine calculator work out the amounts.

What about test strips?

Strips are quick for a daily check, but coarse: on a typical 7-way strip the alkalinity pads jump 40 ppm at a time, and reading colours by eye is the most common complaint. Before you dose from a strip, confirm with drops. Tubwise lets you tap the number printed on the strip bottle instead of judging colour on a screen, and it caps strip-based doses at half.
