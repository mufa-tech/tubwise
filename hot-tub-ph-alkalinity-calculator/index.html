How it’s worked out

Why alkalinity changes the pH dose

Total alkalinity (TA) is the water’s buffer. The more buffer, the more acid it takes to move pH. Label rates like “10 g per 1,000 L lowers pH by 0.1” ignore TA, so they can be off by a factor of three in a soft-water tub.

The acid model

Tubwise uses the TroubleFreePool Pool Math acid model, written in milliequivalents so any acid strength works:

p̄ = (pH now + pH target) ÷ 2
adj = (192.1626 − 60.1221 p̄ + 6.0752 p̄² − 0.1943 p̄³) × (TA + 13.91) ÷ 114.6
acid (meq/L) = ΔpH × (adj + extra × borate) × 0.325469

31.45 % muriatic acid holds 10.006 meq per mL; dry acid (93.2 % sodium bisulfate) 7.763 meq per g. Soda ash and borax use the same buffer term in the other direction.

Safety steps

pH moves at most 0.3 per step, and each step is worked out at its own mid-point. The calculator shows 75 % of the first step: add that, run the jets for 20 minutes, test again. Acid stops before TA would fall below 50 ppm, because pH swings when there is too little buffer left.

Hot tubs lose CO₂ fast at 37 to 40 °C, so pH drifts up after dosing. That is normal; retest instead of chasing the exact number.

Baking soda for alkalinity

Baking soda (sodium bicarbonate, 84.007 g/mol) adds one equivalent of alkalinity per mole:

grams = TA rise ÷ 50.04 × litres × 84.007 ÷ 1000

Each step raises TA by at most 30 ppm, and 75 % of the first step is shown. Example: 1,000 L from 60 to 80 ppm is 33.6 g in total; add 25.2 g, then test.

In the app

Tubwise takes these numbers from your test, puts the steps in order (alkalinity, then pH, then sanitizer) and starts a retest timer after each one.
