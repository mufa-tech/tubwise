How it’s worked out

The factor

DPD reagents react with bromine and chlorine molecule for molecule. A bromine molecule (Br₂, 159.808 g/mol) weighs 2.2538 times a chlorine molecule (Cl₂, 70.906 g/mol), so the same colour means 2.25 times as many ppm of bromine.

bromine ppm = chlorine-scale ppm × 2.25

Taylor rounds the factor to 2.25, and so do Tubwise and this page, so the numbers match the kit charts.

The ranges are not the same

Chlorine tubs aim for 3 to 5 ppm free chlorine; bromine tubs for 4 to 6 ppm total bromine (PHTA). The maximum is 10 ppm for both. Do not convert a chlorine range to bromine: 3 to 5 ppm chlorine would be 6.75 to 11.25 on the bromine scale, and the top of that is past the bromine maximum.

Bromine banks and shock

With a sodium bromide reserve in the water, any oxidiser (MPS or chlorine) turns bromide back into bromine: 1 ppm of chlorine makes 2.25 ppm of bromine.
