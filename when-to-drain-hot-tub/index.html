How it’s worked out

The rule

The CDC hot tub module gives the water replacement interval as:

days = (volume in US gallons ÷ 3) ÷ average bathers per day

In litres that is days = litres ÷ (11.36 × bathers per day). A bather is one person in one soak. Example: 400 gallons and 2 bathers a day: (400 ÷ 3) ÷ 2 = 66.7, so every 66 days.

Rounding and the 90-day cap

The result is rounded down and never more than 90 days. Even a tub that is hardly used needs fresh water about every 3 months: dissolved solids and CYA build up and the water gets harder to keep clear.

TDS

Total dissolved solids (TDS) is the other trigger. When the tub’s TDS is more than 1,500 ppm above your fill water (PHTA), drain now. Whichever comes first wins.

Other rules

Ontario’s public spa rule, litres ÷ (10 × bathers), gives intervals about 14 % longer. Tubwise uses the shorter US rule.
